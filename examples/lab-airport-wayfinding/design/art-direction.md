# Art direction — Calm route system

## Design brief

- **Subject and central claim:** Airport wayfinding succeeds under pressure by structuring attention into destinations, decisions, sequence, stable cues, redundancy, and confirmation. Rushed product interfaces should do the same.
- **Source material:** The approved brief and story contract only. All examples and diagrams are original conceptual evidence; no external research, assets, brands, named airports, or quantitative claims.
- **Dominant content types:** Routes, junctions, information horizons, repeated sign tokens, cue systems, comparison mappings, a decision-map process, and a checklist.
- **Audience and action:** Product designers and product managers should recognize rushed use as a distinct condition and apply six principles plus a pre-ship glance test.
- **Venue and surfaces:** A 15-minute presenter-led room talk on the locked 1600 × 900 canvas, with readable audience, speaker, document, export, and website endpoints.
- **Accessibility:** Large room-readable type, high contrast, redundant color/shape/text cues, no meaning carried by motion alone, deterministic reduced-motion and export endpoints.
- **Tone and pacing:** Calm authority under pressure. Concise visible slides; explanation and caveats live in speaker notes.

## Locked visual foundation

- Canvas: 1600 × 900, 16:9, unchanged from the approved brief and `drever.config.ts`.
- Hard safe area: 56 px on every edge.
- Default content inset: 96 px left/right and 72 px top/bottom.
- Theme owner: `design/theme.ts` and `design/theme.css` own canvas paint, typography, semantic Markdown, color, spacing, content surfaces, and motion tokens.
- Stage background owner: `design/stage-background.tsx` owns the stationary route grid, persistent low-contrast route line, and chapter atmosphere.
- Persistent accent owner: the Theme owns the yellow destination token and blue directional route language; slides do not introduce competing rails or frames.
- Last-known-good checkpoint: the source-clean content-complete Draft 1 created after approval.

## Visual premise and evidence

**Visual premise:** The deck behaves like a calm route system: every scene gives the eye one destination, one navigable relationship, and a settled endpoint.

- Subject-led forms: destination chips, route lines, junctions, horizon markers, confirmation pips, stable labels, and decision gates.
- Subject-led color roles: deep midnight canvas for urgency and orientation, warm off-white reading surfaces, blue for route/action, yellow for destination/attention, muted blue-gray for secondary context.
- Fallback choice: system sans-serif typography is used for reliable local rendering and export. It is a restrained fallback, not a claim about airport typography.
- No external assets or licenses are required.

## Type system

- Family: `Inter, ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif` for display, body, labels, and numerics.
- Source/license: system fallback stack; no downloaded font asset.
- Display: 68–92 px, 800–900 weight, compact leading, editorial line breaks preserving clauses.
- Titles: 60–76 px, 800–900 weight.
- Body and visible explanatory copy: 28–36 px, 650–800 weight.
- Functional labels: 22–26 px, 750–850 weight. Small all-caps labels are limited to short navigation roles.
- Numerics: tabular figures where route steps or principle numbers appear.

## Color roles

- Canvas / primary ink: `#0b1f33` / `#f7f4ea`.
- Elevated light surface / dark ink: `#f7f4ea` / `#10233a`.
- Route/action accent: `#2f6fed`.
- Destination/attention signal: `#ffd34e`.
- Quiet context: `#9aabba` on dark and `#5c6f82` on light.
- Close inversion: the same midnight canvas with a stronger yellow route signal; no new accent is introduced.

## Visual-story pattern

- **ID:** `route-constraint-gate-decision`.
- **Rationale:** The story begins with a route under pressure, exposes attention as the constraint, reveals six causal gates, and ends in an actionable review decision.
- **Beat mapping:** establish route (1–3) → expose route rules (4–11) → translate the system (12) → attach design gates (13–14) → decide and resolve (15).
- **Recurring focal artifact:** a square yellow destination token connected by a blue route line. It appears only where destination, route, or decision continuity is the evidence.
- **Final static payoff:** destination → decision → confirmation resolves into “Design for the glance. Reward the read.”

## Signature moments

1. **Pressure becomes a route** — Claim: clarity converts a rushed state into a confident next step. Focal artifact: a yellow destination token and blue route line. Initial state: title and destination token are settled. Meaningful transformation: one route line draws toward the destination while a signal travels to its endpoint. Settled payoff: “Rushed interfaces → confident next steps.” Reduced-motion/export: the complete route is visible with the signal docked at the endpoint.
2. **A fork becomes a sequence** — Claim: one current decision should create the context for the next question. Focal artifact: the fixed 72 × 72 yellow decision token shared by slides 6 and 7. Initial state: token marks the active fork. Meaningful transformation: fixed-shell continuity carries it into the first position of the four-stage sequence; the route signal then advances locally through the ordered stages. Settled payoff: all four questions are visible in causal order. Reduced-motion/export: the token is already docked at stage one and the full sequence is complete.
3. **Method becomes a test** — Claim: the decision map turns into a practical pre-ship gate. Focal artifact: the final 64 × 64 method checkpoint is reinterpreted as the first review gate. Initial state: checkpoint 04 closes the four-step method. Meaningful transformation: a direct settled scene change preserves the checkpoint grammar without capturing it; the next Step replaces the single prompt with the six-question review artifact. Settled payoff: six complete review questions. Reduced-motion/export: the first gate and all checklist rows are fully visible.
4. **The route resolves** — Claim: immediate clarity and available depth are complementary. Focal artifact: destination → decision → confirmation route. Initial state: three named stops are present. Meaningful transformation: a single signal crosses the route and the Stage atmosphere shifts to the closing state. Settled payoff: the final rule and complete route hold together. Reduced-motion/export: the route is complete and the final rule is fully readable.

## Motion vocabulary

- Direct cuts are the default for unrelated or geometry-incompatible edges.
- Native continuity is limited to the two fixed-shell objects documented above.
- Local route signals travel only along an authored path and stop at a real endpoint.
- Ordered groups reveal once in reading order; they remain fully settled for reduced motion, document, and export.
- No full-canvas snapshot motion; the Stage root is stationary.

### Map implementation notes

- Every Scene-map focal artifact is present in `slides.mdx` and styled by `deck.css`; the Stage remains transparent to slide content and owns all persistent canvas paint.
- The `6→7` Handoff-map edge is the only native continuity sequence and uses the documented fixed-size token shell. Rendered evidence moved `13→14` to a direct semantic-successor cut because its travel path crossed readable copy.
- Geometry-incompatible semantic successors use settled local scenes or direct changes; they do not stretch text, panels, or route geometry to imply continuity.
- The opening, comparison emphasis, and closing route animate only while their slide is active. Reduced-motion, document, and export surfaces receive the complete settled endpoint.
