<div className="pm-slide">
  <div className="pm-topline">
    <p className="pm-kicker">01 / Generate</p>
    <span className="pm-tag">2 minutes · silent</span>
  </div>

  <h2>Start from failure, not anxiety.</h2>

  <div className="pm-prompt" style={{ marginTop: '2rem' }}>
    <blockquote>“It is two weeks after launch. It failed. What happened?”</blockquote>
  </div>

  <Step at={1}>
    <div className="pm-sector-grid" aria-label="Four reusable lenses for generating failure stories">
      <div className="pm-sector"><strong>Value</strong><span>People did not get the promised outcome.</span></div>
      <div className="pm-sector"><strong>Adoption</strong><span>People could not start or keep using it.</span></div>
      <div className="pm-sector"><strong>Delivery</strong><span>The system or operation could not carry the load.</span></div>
      <div className="pm-sector"><strong>Trust</strong><span>The launch damaged confidence, safety, or compliance.</span></div>
    </div>
  </Step>

  <Note>
    Facilitate this as silent writing first. Ask for short failure headlines, one per note, and explicitly prohibit solutions for these two minutes. Silence prevents the first confident speaker from setting the range of acceptable concern. If the room stalls, reveal the four lenses: value, adoption, delivery, and trust. They are prompts, not categories the team must fill evenly. Ask for observed outcomes—“people abandon setup”—rather than abstractions like “bad UX.”
  </Note>
</div>

<div className="pm-slide">
  <div className="pm-topline">
    <p className="pm-kicker">02 / Clarify</p>
    <span className="pm-tag">6 minutes · share + cluster</span>
  </div>
  <h2>Turn failure stories into risk signals.</h2>

  <div className="pm-flow" aria-label="A three-part flow from failure story to cause to observable signal">
    <div className="pm-flow-card">
      <span className="pm-flow-index">FAILURE</span>
      <h3>What went wrong?</h3>
      <p>State the visible outcome, without blame.</p>
    </div>
    <Step at={1}>
      <div className="pm-flow-card">
        <span className="pm-flow-index">CAUSE</span>
        <h3>Because what?</h3>
        <p>Name the condition the team can influence.</p>
      </div>
    </Step>
    <Step at={2}>
      <div className="pm-flow-card">
        <span className="pm-flow-index">SIGNAL</span>
        <h3>How would we know?</h3>
        <p className="pm-formula">When ___, we expect ___.</p>
      </div>
    </Step>
  </div>

  <Note>
    Have each person read one headline at a time; cluster only obvious duplicates. For any vague item, use the three prompts in order. “What went wrong?” keeps the statement outcome-based. “Because what?” moves toward a controllable condition without turning the session into solutioning. “How would we know?” creates an observable trigger. Keep the pace brisk: clarification should sharpen a risk, not litigate whether it is true. Park solutions in a separate area for later.
  </Note>
</div>

        <span className="pm-dot pm-dot-3" aria-label="Selected risk 3">3</span>
      </Step>
    </div>
  </div>

  <Note>
    Draw a simple two-by-two: likelihood left to right, impact bottom to top. Place each clustered risk by quick consent; do not score decimals or debate exact boundaries. If views differ, place the item at the more exposed position and flag the uncertainty. Then apply the forcing function: choose exactly three from the high-exposure region. Break ties by asking, “Which risk becomes cheaper if we act before launch?” The visual reveal numbers only three contacts, turning an unranked field into a decision.
  </Note>
</div>

If assigning one row takes more than two minutes, the risk is still too broad: split or rewrite it.
