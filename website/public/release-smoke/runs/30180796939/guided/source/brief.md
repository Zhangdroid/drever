# Presentation brief

## Goal

Enable a small group of product, design, and engineering leads to choose the three pre-launch risks that deserve named owners.

## Audience

Cross-functional product, design, and engineering leads preparing for a product launch. They need a reusable facilitation method rather than a fictional case study.

## Content

- Frame the pre-mortem: assume the launch failed, then work backward.
- Generate failure stories independently before group discussion.
- Convert stories into specific risk signals.
- Rank risks by likelihood and impact, not by who argues most loudly.
- Select exactly three risks and assign an owner, trigger, and first action.
- Close with a reusable 20-minute agenda.

## Direction

- Duration: 8 minutes
- Language: English
- Slide density: concise with fuller facilitation notes
- Motion intensity: expressive but purposeful
- Visual voice: calm launch control / risk radar; dark navy surfaces, cyan radar geometry, amber risk signals, coral escalation accents
- Visual references or official sources: none required; use an original local abstraction
- Motion or interaction references and tools: presenter-driven `Step` reveals plus bounded local CSS transitions; no continuous ambient motion
- Provided or permitted assets: local MDX, CSS, and SVG/CSS geometry only
- Deliverables: live presentation source

## Assumptions

- The talk introduces the method; the actual team exercise happens afterward in a 20-minute session.
- No product-specific examples are used, so the method remains reusable.
- Three is a deliberate forcing function: enough coverage to matter, few enough to own.
