# Art direction: Mending the Night

**Visual premise:** A dark-sky field guide in which every amber mark represents light with a job, while the blue-black negative space is treated as a designed material.

## System

- Type: system sans for spoken claims; system monospace for labels and audit language.
- Palette: ink night (`#07111f`), deep blue (`#10243a`), moon paper (`#f2f5ed`), task amber (`#ffbd59`), cool cyan (`#75d5df`).
- Grid: generous editorial margins with diagrams occupying the central field.
- Motif: a thin horizon and sparse star points; decoration stays quiet until shielding makes darkness visible.
- Motion voice: stillness first, then one presenter-controlled optical correction.
- Assets: no external assets, marks, fonts, or imagery.

## Signature moment

1. **Useful light has a boundary** → streetlamp distribution diagram → an exposed lamp throws light upward and sideways → a Step adds a shield and replaces the halo with a downward task cone → the ground remains legible while the sky regains stars → the reduced-motion/static endpoint is the fully shielded lamp with the downward cone.

The descending night clock is a quieter supporting composition, not a second motion event. Its complete static profile lets the audience read the evening-to-dawn reduction without competing with the optical transformation.

## Composition rhythm

Opening poster → full-canvas optical transformation → radial audit tool → paired contrast comparison → horizontal timeline → spare closing instruction.

## Anti-patterns

- No generic neon-tech gradient.
- No decorative motion in the star field.
- No tiny pseudo-technical copy.
- No claim that more or less light automatically guarantees safety.
