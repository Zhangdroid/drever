# Presentation brief

## Goal

Persuade civic designers, facilities teams, and neighborhood leaders to treat darkness as part of the lighting design. The audience should leave able to audit one block and replace blanket brightness with light that has a defined job, place, time, and limit.

## Audience

Mixed, non-specialist decision-makers who influence streets, campuses, storefronts, or public spaces. No lighting-engineering background is assumed.

## Content

- Central claim: good outdoor lighting puts useful light where it is needed and preserves darkness elsewhere.
- Demonstrate how shielding changes a lamp's distribution.
- Introduce four questions: need, target, timing, and amount.
- Contrast blanket brightness with designed contrast.
- Show a simple evening-to-dawn operating rhythm.
- End with a concrete one-block night audit.

## Direction

- Duration: 7 minutes
- Language: English
- Slide density: concise with fuller speaker notes
- Motion intensity: restrained; one meaningful Step transformation
- Visual voice: deep-night field guide; ink-blue sky, warm amber task light, sparse constellations, technical annotations
- Visual references or official sources: none; original local abstractions only
- Motion or interaction references and tools: native Drever Steps and CSS
- Provided or permitted assets: none
- Deliverables: live presentation and website; no PDF in this generation stage
