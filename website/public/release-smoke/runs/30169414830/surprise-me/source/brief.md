# Presentation brief

## Goal

Help product and operations teams redesign one AI-assisted workflow so human attention is reserved for the moment an action becomes consequential, rather than scattered across every generated step.

## Audience

Product managers, designers, operations leads, and engineers who already use AI-assisted drafting or automation and need a practical pattern for deciding where human review belongs.

## Content

- Central claim: the safest useful AI workflows do not pause everywhere; they pause at the boundary where a reversible suggestion becomes an external action.
- Introduce a simple decision model using reversibility and blast radius.
- Trace one artifact through draft, commit, and broadcast boundaries.
- Give the audience a four-question checkpoint contract.
- End with a 15-minute exercise that can be applied to a real workflow the next day.
- This is a conceptual operating model, not an empirical benchmark; no statistics or external evidence are claimed.

## Direction

- Duration: 7 minutes
- Language: English
- Slide density: balanced, with concise speaker notes
- Motion intensity: expressive but restrained; two presenter-led reveals on one workflow slide
- Visual voice: industrial safety signage meets systems diagramming
- Visual references or official sources: none; original local design
- Motion or interaction references and tools: Drever Steps only
- Provided or permitted assets: local CSS and authored HTML shapes only
- Deliverables: live presentation and website; no PDF in this generation stage

## Assumptions

- The audience wants a reusable decision framework rather than implementation code.
- The venue is a normal meeting room or video call, so contrast and large type take priority over dense detail.
- The closing action should be useful without a link or external worksheet.
