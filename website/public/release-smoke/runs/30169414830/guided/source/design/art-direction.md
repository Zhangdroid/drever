# Art direction: launch-control risk radar

## Visual premise

Treat a pre-mortem as a calm signal-acquisition system: the room starts with scattered weak echoes, converts them into comparable risk tracks, and ends with three confirmed controls.

## Subject-led system

- **Palette:** midnight operations field; pale instrument text; cyan for observed signals; amber for uncertainty; coral only for the three committed risks.
- **Type:** system sans for distance readability and system mono for prompts, coordinates, timing, and owner fields. No remote fonts.
- **Grid:** wide instrument panels, circular radar geometry, and horizontal process tracks. Compositions change by idea rather than repeating a card split.
- **Motif:** concentric range rings and crosshairs. Rings are evidence and comparison scaffolds, not decoration.
- **Motion:** one-shot acquisition sweeps, signal convergence, and final target lock. Steps reveal decisions; ambient elements do not loop. Reduced motion shows the final state immediately.

## Signature moments

1. Opening radar turns an imagined failed launch into visible weak signals.
2. A failure story is translated into a testable risk track with an earliest signal.
3. A crowded risk field resolves to exactly three confirmed targets, then hands those targets to owner controls.

## Quiet supporting moments

- A single 25-minute operations track explains the facilitation sequence.
- A compact owner-card close makes the next action concrete.

## Principles

- Make the decision visible: three risks, not a comprehensive register.
- Use sparse labels and let geometry carry sequence and comparison.
- Reserve high contrast for the current claim.
- Keep facilitator detail in notes.

## Anti-patterns

- Generic gradient cards or decorative dashboards.
- Continuous radar loops that compete with reading.
- Probability theater or false precision.
- Reveals that move persistent titles or controls.
- Risk lists without owners, signals, or a launch-time trigger.

## Assets and licensing

No external assets, marks, imagery, or fonts. All visual forms are original CSS and inline semantic markup.
