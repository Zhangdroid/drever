Read the ladder quickly, without turning it into trivia. Moonlight is a little over a second old. Sunlight left before this sentence began. Sirius is a childhood photograph on a cosmic scale. Andromeda’s light began crossing space before our species existed. The values are rounded for spoken clarity.
</Note>

The dark lines are the fingerprint. The same pattern appears in both bars, but every observed line has moved toward the red end. That displacement is evidence, not an artist’s metaphor: astronomers compare known spectral features with what arrives. Keep the explanation qualitative; the purpose is to show how old light carries both an image of its source and a record of its journey.
</Note>

<Note>
Stars had not yet formed when this light was released. Roughly 380,000 years after the Big Bang, electrons and nuclei combined into neutral atoms, and photons could travel freely. We observe that relic today as the cosmic microwave background. The timeline is conceptual rather than linear-to-scale; its job is to isolate the release event inside a 13.8-billion-year story.
</Note>
