# Presentation brief

## Goal

Give a general audience one memorable mental model: looking farther into space means looking farther into the past. End with a simple observing ritual they can use the same night.

## Audience

Curious non-specialists. No astronomy background is assumed.

## Content

- Light has a finite speed, so every astronomical view is delayed.
- Familiar lookback times: Moon 1.3 seconds, Sun 8 minutes 20 seconds, Sirius 8.6 years, Andromeda roughly 2.5 million years.
- Redshift lets astronomers read cosmic expansion from stretched wavelengths.
- The cosmic microwave background is the oldest light we can observe, released when the universe became transparent roughly 380,000 years after the Big Bang.
- Close with a concrete invitation to look up and name the age of the light arriving now.

## Direction

- Duration: 6 minutes
- Language: English
- Slide density: balanced, with concise visible copy and fuller speaker notes
- Motion intensity: restrained; presenter-driven reveals only where sequence changes understanding
- Visual voice: night observatory meets scientific instrument; ink-black space, spectral cyan, and photon amber
- Visual references or official sources: none required for this generation-only pass
- Motion or interaction references and tools: native Drever Steps and local CSS only
- Provided or permitted assets: original local CSS and SVG only; no external assets
- Deliverables: live presentation source

## Assumptions

Surprise mode authorizes the topic, audience, timing, density, motion, and visual direction. Numerical values are intentionally rounded for spoken clarity.
