# Art direction: Pre-mortem launch control

## Visual premise

Treat the pre-mortem as a calm launch-control scan: faint radar geometry establishes the search space, while bright coordinates appear only when the group makes a decision.

## Subject-led system

- **Palette:** near-black navy for focus, cool teal for observed signals, amber for unresolved risk, and warm white for decisions.
- **Typography:** sturdy system sans-serif for room readability; tabular monospace for time, coordinates, and operational labels.
- **Grid:** a 12-column console with large quiet margins and thin instrument rules.
- **Motif:** concentric radar rings and crosshairs. Rings stay geometrically consistent; emphasis comes from a single highlighted sector or point.
- **Motion voice:** presenter-controlled state commits. Reveals indicate a new decision, never decorative entrance.

## Signature moments

1. The opening radar locates one question: “What could make this launch fail?”
2. The risk map turns many concerns into a shared impact/evidence coordinate system.
3. The closing ownership board resolves exactly three amber contacts into named commitments.

## Quiet slides

The eight-minute protocol and failure-writing prompt use restrained console layouts so the decision slides carry the visual emphasis.

## Accessibility and delivery

- High-contrast type on opaque surfaces; color is reinforced by labels, position, and shape.
- No remote fonts or assets.
- No continuous animation; all meaningful changes are presenter-driven Steps.
- Decorative geometry is hidden from assistive technology; diagrams include concise accessible descriptions.

## Anti-patterns

- No fictional launch, invented metrics, or category quotas.
- No red “danger wall,” generic gradient cards, or dense dashboard chrome.
- No motion on persistent titles, rules, or radar geometry.
