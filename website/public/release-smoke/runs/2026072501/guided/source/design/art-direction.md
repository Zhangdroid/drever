# Art direction — Launch pre-mortem

## Visual premise

Treat the pre-mortem as a calm launch-control exercise: scan a noisy field of possible failure signals, resolve them into testable chains, and commit three warnings to owners before the countdown reaches zero.

## Subject-led system

- **Palette:** near-black graphite suggests a control-room surface; amber marks unresolved warnings; mint marks decisions and ownership; warm white carries the spoken argument.
- **Typography:** a compact system sans for decisive headings and a system monospace for timers, labels, and risk IDs.
- **Grid:** strong horizontal telemetry tracks, faint radar rings, and aligned checkpoints make sequence and decision criteria visible.
- **Recurring motif:** a thin countdown rail advances from “imagine failure” to “assign owners,” while a low-contrast risk radar stays fixed behind the content. Both remain subordinate and stationary within each slide.
- **Risk artifacts:** off-white slips turn abstract worries into concrete objects that can be sorted, scored, and owned.
- **Motion:** expressive but purposeful. Four presenter-owned `Step` moments carry a single job each: lock the room onto the failure prompt, fan out the three functional lenses, turn one vague concern into a testable risk slip, and commit exactly three risks to owner slots. Persistent titles, radar rings, and countdown rails stay still.

## Signature moments

1. A field of warning signals converges on three owned risks on the opening slide.
2. The room shifts from one shared failure frame to three cross-functional scanning lenses.
3. A vague worry resolves into a cause → failure → impact chain, then three risks cross the ownership threshold.

## Quieter supporting slides

The failure frame, silent-generation lanes, and closing 25-minute runbook use sparse diagrams and short operational labels.

## Accessibility and surfaces

- High-contrast text and warning colors are never the sole carrier of meaning.
- SVG diagrams include accessible titles and descriptions.
- The final state is fully understandable without motion.
- No remote fonts, imagery, media, or runtime dependencies.

## Fallback choices

System fonts are used because external font loading is prohibited. The visual language is an original abstraction, not a copy of an aerospace or product brand.
