# Presentation brief

## Goal

Help a small cross-functional launch team choose the three risks that deserve named owners before a product launch.

## Audience

Product, design, and engineering leads. They understand their launch context and need a fast, practical decision method rather than a general argument for risk management.

## Content

- Define a useful pre-mortem as a rehearsal of failure, not a forecast.
- Establish psychological safety: assume failure, generate silently, and delay solutioning.
- Turn vague worries into testable chains: cause → failure → impact.
- Select risks using three filters: material impact, plausible evidence, and weak or late detection.
- Finish with exactly three owned risks, each with a trigger, owner, and next mitigation.
- Include a 25-minute facilitation runbook in the closing slide and detailed guidance in speaker notes.

## Direction

- Duration: 8 minutes
- Language: English
- Slide density: concise with fuller speaker notes
- Motion intensity: expressive but purposeful; four presenter-driven reveals advance one concrete artifact at a time through the facilitation sequence
- Visual voice: calm launch-control and risk-radar abstraction—graphite field, amber warnings, mint confirmation, crisp telemetry lines, radar rings, and paper-like risk slips
- Visual references or official sources: none; no external research or assets in this isolated generation stage
- Motion or interaction references and tools: native Drever `Step` only
- Provided or permitted assets: local CSS and inline SVG only
- Deliverables: authored Drever presentation source

## Assumptions

- The talk teaches a reusable facilitation method rather than reviewing a specific launch.
- The team can schedule a separate 25-minute pre-mortem.
- The facilitator will capture risks in a shared workspace after the talk.
- Motion should make the facilitation sequence legible: establish the failure frame, fan out functional lenses, convert one worry into a testable risk, then commit three risks to owners.
