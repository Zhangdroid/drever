# Art direction: The checkout ledger

**Visual premise:** the team is reading one honest checkout receipt: value is recorded in green ink, operational cost in coral, and every unresolved assumption remains visibly unfilled.

## Subject-led choices

- Receipt edges, ledger rules, tabular numerals, and compact labels come from the checkout subject.
- Green is reserved for confirmed completion improvement; coral is reserved for the confirmed rise in support volume.
- Warm paper and near-black ink keep the room focused on a practical product decision rather than a generic technology aesthetic.
- A single vertical “decision rail” recurs as the deck’s quiet motif.

## Signature moments

1. The opening pairs the two observed changes as one indivisible transaction.
2. The completion slide turns 100 checkout starts into two directly comparable horizontal outcomes.
3. The ramp slide converts uncertainty into a concrete sequence with explicit promotion gates.

## Rhythm and motion

Five slides: tension, benefit, cost, controlled action, decision. Navigation should feel like advancing through a ledger. No local animation is necessary; the metrics and decision sequence provide the movement.

## Fallback choices

- System sans and monospace fonts are used because external fonts are forbidden.
- The proposed rollout thresholds are facilitation defaults and must be confirmed by the team.

## Anti-patterns

- Do not celebrate 81% without showing the doubled support count.
- Do not compare raw support counts with completion percentages as if they share a denominator.
- Do not use gradients, decorative dashboards, or motion that competes with the decision.
