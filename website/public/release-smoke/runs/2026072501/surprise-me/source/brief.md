# Presentation brief

## Goal

Help a product team decide whether to expand a fictional checkout experiment that improved completion while increasing support demand.

## Audience

A cross-functional product room familiar with checkout metrics and able to approve, instrument, and stop a staged rollout.

## Content

- Checkout completion improved from 68% to 81%.
- Weekly support requests increased from 6 to 12.
- The recommendation is a guarded expansion, not an immediate full rollout.
- Raw weekly support counts must be normalized to experiment exposure before the team interprets the increase as a rate.
- The team should classify issue severity and confirm statistical credibility before expanding.

## Direction

- Duration: 6 minutes
- Language: English
- Slide density: concise with fuller speaker notes
- Motion intensity: restrained; no authored Steps are required for the decision
- Visual voice: an original checkout-receipt ledger—warm paper, dark ink, success green, caution coral, precise tabular numerals
- Visual references or official sources: none; the experiment and product are fictional
- Motion or interaction references and tools: native Drever navigation only
- Provided or permitted assets: local CSS and authored shapes only; no external assets
- Deliverables: live presentation source

## Assumptions and judgment calls

- The team can run a 50% traffic stage and review it after two weeks.
- The thresholds shown are proposed decision guardrails, not findings from the supplied data.
- “Critical issue” means a payment failure, duplicate charge, or customer lockout requiring immediate rollback.
- Sample size, confidence interval, exposure by variant, support severity, and support rate per 1,000 checkout attempts were not supplied and are treated as unresolved.
