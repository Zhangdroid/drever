# Presentation brief

## Goal

Help a general audience see a traffic signal as a public time budget, then give them a simple way to evaluate an intersection: ask who receives time, who waits, and whose delay is being counted.

## Audience

Curious non-specialists: residents, designers, students, and civic teams. No transport-engineering knowledge is assumed.

## Content

- A signal cycle allocates a finite number of seconds among movements.
- Waiting should be considered in person-seconds, not only vehicle delay.
- Coordinating adjacent signals can create a “green wave” at a chosen travel speed.
- The closing action is to observe one familiar intersection and audit who gets the next second.
- All quantities in the deck are explicitly illustrative rather than claims about a real junction.

## Direction

- Duration: 6 minutes
- Language: English
- Slide density: concise with fuller speaker notes
- Motion intensity: restrained; presenter-driven Steps reveal only consequential comparisons
- Visual voice: nighttime street markings, signal lenses, timing diagrams, and precise civic-infrastructure typography
- Visual references or official sources: none; original local abstractions only
- Motion or interaction references and tools: Drever Steps; no external tools
- Provided or permitted assets: local CSS and authored SVG only
- Deliverables: live presentation source; no PDF in this generation stage

## Assumptions

The presentation is delivered in a dim or mixed-light room on a 16:9 canvas. The audience should leave with a new mental model rather than a technical signal-timing recipe.
