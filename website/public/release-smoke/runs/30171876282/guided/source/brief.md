# Presentation brief

## Goal

Enable a small group of product, design, and engineering leads to run a useful launch pre-mortem and choose exactly three risks that deserve named owners before launch.

## Audience

Cross-functional product, design, and engineering leads. They know their product and launch plan; they need a fast, reusable decision method rather than an introduction to risk management.

## Content

- Frame the pre-mortem as working backward from an imagined failed launch.
- Use a reusable 20-minute facilitation sequence: frame, silent generation, clustering, selection, ownership.
- Generate observable failure evidence rather than vague concerns.
- Select risks with two passes: severity plus plausibility, then ability to change the outcome before launch.
- Convert the three selected risks into explicit owner contracts with an action, signal, and revisit date.
- Do not invent a fictional product or launch.

## Direction

- Duration: 8 minutes
- Language: English
- Slide density: concise with fuller speaker notes
- Motion intensity: expressive but purposeful
- Visual voice: calm launch control / risk radar; dark technical surfaces, precise rings, restrained amber and mint signals
- Visual references or official sources: none required; original local abstraction only
- Motion or interaction references and tools: native Drever Steps and local CSS; motion explains scanning, sequence, and decision commitment
- Provided or permitted assets: no external assets; local CSS and SVG-free geometric forms only
- Deliverables: live presentation source; no PDF requested

## Assumptions

- The group has a facilitator and can use separate notes or a shared board during the exercise.
- The pre-mortem itself is a 20-minute working session; the deck explains it in 8 minutes.
- The deck should work for any product launch and therefore uses prompts and templates rather than sample launch content.
