<Note>
**Timing: 1 minute 15 seconds.** Advance once per phase. The sequence matters: private generation before group discussion prevents the loudest function from defining the risk landscape.

Facilitation script: “For twenty minutes, we are not fixing everything. First we expose signals, then compress them, then choose, then assign.”

Keep the timeboxes visible. If the group starts solving during writing or clustering, capture the proposed action off to the side and return to the current phase. The last three minutes are protected; do not let discussion consume ownership time.
</Note>

<Note>
**Timing: 1 minute 30 seconds.** Read the prompts, then explain the writing standard. “Adoption might be bad” is a concern. “The target user cannot complete the first critical task” is observable failure evidence.

Ask each person to write across functions, not only inside their discipline. Aim for roughly three to five notes per person. Anonymous notes are useful when hierarchy is strong.

During clustering, participants silently move similar notes together first. Then the facilitator gives each cluster a short failure label that describes the outcome, not a root-cause theory. Keep genuinely distinct failure modes separate even if one mitigation could affect both.
</Note>

<Note>
**Timing: 2 minutes.** This is the decision slide.

Pass 1: place clusters quickly using two questions: “If this happened, how much would it damage the launch outcome?” and “Given what we know today, how plausible is it?” Use relative placement, not invented numeric precision. The upper-right is the candidate set.

Advance for Pass 2. Ask of each candidate: “Can a named person materially change the likelihood, impact, or detection time before launch?” Actionability does not make a risk more important; it determines the response. If no, log it for monitoring or escalation rather than pretending an owner can control it.

If more than three remain, give each lead three votes, with no more than two votes on one risk. Resolve ties with the question: “Which risk becomes expensive fastest if we wait?” Cap the selection at three. The cap forces focus and makes ownership credible.
</Note>

      </div>

<Note>
**Timing: 1 minute 30 seconds.** Complete this contract three times, once for each selected risk.

The owner is not necessarily the person doing every task; they are accountable for driving the risk down or surfacing a decision. Use a person’s name, not “Engineering,” “Design,” or a committee.

The action must happen before launch and should change likelihood, impact, or detection time. The early signal needs a threshold, not “monitor metrics.” The revisit date is when the leads decide whether the current launch plan still holds.

If the group cannot name an action, the risk may be a monitoring item rather than an owned mitigation. If no one can own it, that is an escalation—not an empty field to fix later.
</Note>

<Note>
**Timing: 1 minute.** Close by asking the leads to schedule the 20-minute session before the next irreversible launch decision.

Final line: “The pre-mortem is useful when it changes who does what before launch—not when it produces the longest list of things that could go wrong.”

The facilitator should send the three contracts immediately after the session and place the revisit date on the same calendar used for launch readiness. New risks can enter later only by displacing one of the three or through explicit escalation; this protects the focus the group just created.
</Note>
